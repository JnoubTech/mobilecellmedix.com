iPhone 3-Minute Reboot Loop – Panic Logs and Hardware Faults

June 15, 2025 5 min read

Is your iPhone constantly turning off and restarting while you're using it? If you time it, you might notice that it reboots exactly every 3 minutes.

This is a known hardware protection mechanism. The iPhone has several thermal sensors spread across its internal components (the charging port flex cable, the power button flex cable, the camera, and the battery). The logic board constantly polls these sensors to ensure the device isn't overheating.

Why the 3-Minute Panic?

If the logic board fails to receive data from one of these sensors for exactly 3 minutes, it assumes the sensor is disconnected or the device is critically overheating. To prevent damage or fire risk, iOS deliberately triggers a kernel panic and reboots the phone.

This most commonly happens after a hard drop, water damage, or a botched repair where a flex cable was slightly torn. The two most common culprits are the charging port flex assembly (which contains a microphone and thermal sensor) and the power button/flash flex assembly.

How to Check Your Panic Logs

You can confirm this issue by checking your phone's analytics data:

  1. Go to Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements > Analytics Data.
  2. Scroll down and look for files starting with "panic-full".
  3. Open one and look for the keyword "Missing sensor(s)". This line will often identify the exact sensor that has failed (e.g., "Prs0", "Mic1", "TG0B").

The Fix: Restoring the phone's software will not fix this issue because it is a hardware failure. The specific damaged flex cable or sensor must be replaced.
